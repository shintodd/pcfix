Laptops used to be so easy to fix, like this old Toshiba that has a line going through the middle of the screen. Getting the screen swapped out is so simple, because on this model, all you got to do is disconnect it from power and pull out the removable battery. And then open the system back up and identify the four corner screws, which just need to be unscrewed and removed. This lets you unclip the bezel, which pops right off. Then if you just tilt the screen forward just a little bit, it gives you access to the side screws, which, if removed on both sides, freeze up the display panel so you can lay it flat, making it nice and easy to disconnect the display cable. And as simple as that, your screen is now free. We can actually see that line on the panel itself right there. I freed up the screen in, like, two minutes. Then putting our new screen in is just as simple, just in reverse order. We first need to lay it flat on the keyboard so we can connect our LCD cable. Then if we just lift the screen up, it can be screwed onto the rails. This way, we can reinstall our bezel by just snapping that into place and then reinstalling the screws. Now we can close this up, reinstall the battery, and fire the system right up. And check that out. The screen swapped in about 15 minutes. I can almost guarantee that you can't do that on 99% of modern laptops.
