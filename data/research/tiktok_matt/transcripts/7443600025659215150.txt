Why doesn't the motherboard just recharge the CMOS battery so it never dies? Well, that would just make it too easy. But in all seriousness, it actually has to do with the chemical makeup of CR 2032 batteries that are used on motherboards. These aren't rechargeable. And it also doesn't make a whole lot of sense to make them rechargeable either. Because although they do eventually fail, their lifetime is actually really good for a battery, especially on a motherboard, because they don't pull a lot of power. Because when your computer is actually plugged into the wall and powered, the system doesn't really need to use this battery. It's only really ever when the computer is completely disconnected from the wall, and that's when it needs to start sipping power off the CMOS battery to power the BIOS chip.
