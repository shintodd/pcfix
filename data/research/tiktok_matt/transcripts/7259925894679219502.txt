This laptop could boil water as it instantly spikes to 100 degrees Celsius under any load. So let's tear this system apart to get it fixed. In most cases when you experience really high temperatures like this, there's usually one of 3 issues going on. First, you have a broken fan, which in this case we didn't think was probably going on because we could clearly hear it. Or the fan or heat sink could be full of dust. Or you may have an issue with your Thermal paste. After removing the bottom cover off the laptop, we clearly could see that the fan wasn't the issue, but there was a little bit of dust on it, so I went ahead and blow that out. From there. Just based on the behavior, we decided to go ahead and just re paste the CPU, as likely the Thermal paste was causing the issue. In order to do this, we first removed these 4 screws that secure the heat sink onto the CPU and cleaned off the old Thermal paste with some isopropyl alcohol. From there, I then put some brand new Arctic MX IX Thermal paste on the CPU die and reassemble the system. And with that system now repasted, we fired up the system. See that it was back to idling at a reasonable temperature, and even under a simulated CPU stress test, it only peaked in the mid 80s, which was way better than the system previously performed. And With the CPU no longer a hazard, we can now get this laptop back to the customer.
