Here's how I was able to upgrade this laptop from integrated graphics to a dedicated desktop GPU. So for this model, as it didn't support Thunderbolt, I chose to use this MDOT II the PCI Next adapter. To install this, I first had to remove the back cover of the laptop, disconnect the battery, and then remove the second MDOT II drive as that slot would later be used. Then by taking this M DOT II the PCI adapter, I slotted it into the M DOT II slot and screwed it down. With that now installed, we now have to power up the adapter which requires a 24 pin ATX connector and this 4 pin CPU connector that come from a dedicated power supply. Once I was all connected up, I then soldered in this RTX and plugged in the A 6 and 8 pin Pcie cables that are required. Lastly, in order to get the built in display to actually work, I had to use this display port headless adapter in order to trick the GPU to thinking there was a secondary display. With everything now plugged in, all I had to do was turn on the power supply, plug in the laptop, power, and turn it on. Once Windows loaded up, I grabbed the latest slim video driver and used Microsoft settings to set my games to open during testing using the external GPU.
