A Fusion Drive is just what apple called their hybrid hard drives. The idea of a hybrid hard drive is actually pretty simple. See at the time that these were usually being sold, it was when SSD were really expensive. So a way to go and give you better performance while giving you a good amount of capacity. You can basically get a 1 terabyte Fusion Drive. This would include a 1 terabyte hard drive and usually like a 32 gigabyte SSD and they would work in combination. See how these drives work is that it's all software based. Basically in Mac OS it would determine what applications that you use the most often and basically determine if those should be moved over to the SSD. Once they are moved over to the SSD, this would basically speed up the responsiveness of the entire drive because the computer feel way faster than it would have been if it was running on the hard drive, while still giving you a ton of capacity when it comes to storage. Two reasons you don't see Fusion Drives today or even hybrid hard drives on PCs has to do with the fact that SSDs have gotten incredibly cheap. Because if you go back to 2,012 when Fusion Drives first came out, something like a one terabyte SSD would have cost you well over $500 compared to something like $70 today. The other reason you won't see Fusion Drives today is the high failure rate. And really, that has to do with how they're structured. It's similar to how a RAID 0 set up. Or basically, the data is striped across the two drives, meaning that if one fails, it's gonna actually take down the other with it. So then a ton of data loss for a lot of people. If you look on the forums, or even my own customers, I've seen so many people lose data because of these Fusion Drives. That's just why I don't recommend them.
