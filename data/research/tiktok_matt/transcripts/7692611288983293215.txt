If you're ever building a PC, you'll see that motherboards are typically labeled Mini ITX, Micro ATX, and standard ATX, and on rare occasions, E ATX or extended ATX to signify the form factor that they follow that determines the cases that they're compatible inside of. Something you probably didn't know about these form factors is that Mini ITX is the only one with a locked size, as they always have to be 6.7 inches square. Which is interesting because the original ATX, or an Advanced Technology Extended Form factor originally developed by Intel, the standardized cases with motherboard sizes only cared about the optional mounting screw locations and length of the board being exactly 12 inches, which is very different from the width, which can be as small as 6.7 or as wide as 9.6 inches. But what's even more interesting than these skinny ATX boards is how confusing the micro ATX form factor has become because although the standard is 9.6 inches in length, 6.7 and 9.6 in width, the standard is actually pretty fluid as long as you have the same mounting holes and don't exceed the maximums.
