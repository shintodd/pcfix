The owner of this ancient gaming PC just got screwed by another repair shop. See, they brought their system into another shop with a computer that would actually turn on, but just not display anything out. What they got back was a new GPU and RAM that made the system restart over and over again. So no fix here. And when I went to actually evaluate the system, I realized that the other shop put in RAM that wasn't even compatible with this motherboard. As it's an X58 board, it only supported up to 4 gigabytes, not 16 gigabytes. I don't know what they were thinking here. Plus the issue never went away no matter what I would do either. Basically I tried testing with RAM, even tried a different power supply, reset the CMOS, different RAM slots and configurations, even swapped at the GPU and even the CPU. Which was crazy. I still had something that old that was actually compatible lying around. And every time we got the exact same air code where it would fail the memory training, which is typically associated with an issue with the motherboard. Which is kind of what I thought as soon as the customer told me what was going on. Fortunately, getting an replacement X58 board is 1. Not worth it these days and 2. Is so limited to use boards that are over 15 years old doesn't make sense. So after speaking to the customer about this, we decided our Best plan of action was to take the GPU that they bought from the other shop that was perfectly fine and slot that into a brand new rig. And just like that they went from a system that probably could barely run Windows to a really sold gaming PC. But seriously, how did the shop think it was the RAM? And why would they not check the RAM compatibility? Rookie.
