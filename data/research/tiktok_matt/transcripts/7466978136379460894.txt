Why should you boot the Safe Mode to uninstall your GPU drivers? That's an excellent question, but the best way to think about it is to understand what exactly Safe Mode does. So when you fire up in the Safe Mode, your computer is actually running a minimal version of Windows, meaning just the essentials are actually running. That's why you won't see all your startup apps and a lot of times you can boot the Safe Mode but not the Windows. Maybe a bad startup app or driver is acting up. And speaking of drivers, besides the essential ones, most of them actually don't even load when you go to safe mode. Which is a good thing when you're trying to uninstall a GPU driver, because even though you do need a GPU driver to work, you don't actually have to have the one from Nvidia or AMD for it to actually boot up and display something now. And that's what Safe Mode does because it uses the Microsoft BASIC Display Driver, which is definitely not designed or optimized for gaming or any task. But it does give you a display out long enough to actually work on the computer while you're in Safe Mode. Because the AMD or Nvidia drivers and files don't actually have to be running, it's a lot easier to uninstall them. Cause I'm not sure if you ever tried uninstalling a program or files that were actively running or being used, but it can Lead to a lot of problems like blue screens, instability, or just not responding. So by uninstalling the old GPU drivers in Safe Mode, you don't have to worry about any of that interference. So although you absolutely could try to uninstall your GPU drivers without running in Safe Mode, I wouldn't recommend it.
