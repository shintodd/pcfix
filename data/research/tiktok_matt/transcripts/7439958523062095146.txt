If your PC ever tells you that you have invalid configuration information, or that you need to set the time and date, it likely means that your CMOS battery inside of your PC has failed. So inside your PC you have something called a CR 2032 battery, also known as your bio CMOS battery. This battery is actually in charge of remembering some of these settings for your computer. It doesn't necessarily remember them, it rather just powers a chip on the motherboard that allows your system to remember some of the configuration, how the system was set up, the date and time, or even maybe your boot order. And the reason a battery happens to be used is the fact that if your system didn't have a battery, if it happened to lose power from the wall, or if it got unplugged, everything would just be lost as far as that information for that startup process. But what happens is that when these batteries do fail, when you do lose power, the system can't retain its information. And that's why you get this screen here asking you to basically reset the system up with the basic configuration. It doesn't mean you've lost any of your data necessarily, it just means that you need to go ahead and reset the time. Sometimes you need to reset how your boot order or even some of the other configuration that you may have set up in your system, like XMP profiles. Unfortunately, CR 2032 batteries can be bought pretty much anywhere these days, and if you buy them online they're less than a dollar a piece. So definitely something you may wanna have on hand just in case yours ever goes out.
